# Weekly Menu Pipeline

Worked example for Tuesday, May 5 through Monday, May 11, 2026.

This is based on the personal recipe/import signal rather than direct recipe recreation. The dominant patterns are plant-forward dinners, beans/chickpeas/lentils, tahini/miso/sesame sauces, herby salads and bowls, pasta/noodles, toast/flatbread formats, and occasional spicy harissa/gochujang/chili accents.

## System

The goal is not seven recipes, and it is not generic meal prep. The goal is a chef-style mise graph: every active action, dirty tool, hot oven, washed herb bundle, cooked legume, or sauce base should suggest the next smart branch.

Think of the menu as Tetris. Ingredients, tools, and prep states are the pieces. A good plan fills the week by nesting work efficiently while still making the meals feel distinct.

Each afternoon/evening cooking session should ask:

1. What is dinner tonight?
2. What part of tonight's work naturally becomes tomorrow's lunch?
3. Which tool, station, heat source, or base is already active?
4. What else can pass through that same active setup with minimal extra effort?
5. Will the branch create a genuinely different meal later, not just leftovers?

The planner should not seek "one durable component." It should infer branches from the work already underway.

## Weekly Inputs

Use this order every week:

1. Pick 4 to 6 seasonal produce anchors.
2. Pick 2 protein anchors.
3. Pick 2 starch/carrier anchors.
4. Pick 2 sauces.
5. Pick 1 heat/accent profile.
6. Map each ingredient through at least 2 uses before buying it.

For this week:

- Seasonal produce: cabbage, asparagus, peas, cucumber, radish, strawberries, lemon/lime, herbs, spinach/artichoke, zucchini if it looks good.
- Protein anchors: chickpeas, lentils, edamame, eggs.
- Starch/carriers: pita or sourdough, gnocchi or pasta, couscous/orzo.
- Sauces: lemon-herb vinaigrette, miso-tahini/yogurt sauce.
- Accent profile: sumac, harissa or chili crisp, sesame.

## Mise Graph

Use this state machine for every anchor ingredient:

Raw -> washed/prepped -> cooked or preserved -> remixed -> rescue use

Examples for this week:

- Cabbage: charred wedges -> lunch bowl -> slaw -> lentil salad crunch.
- Asparagus: roasted side -> frittata -> gnocchi/pasta -> cold orzo salad.
- Peas/edamame: blanched vegetable -> edamame smash -> salad protein -> snack dip.
- Chickpeas: warm salad -> falafel/hummus -> crispy chickpea croutons.
- Cucumber/radish: snack tray -> quick pickle -> pita crunch -> salad base.
- Herbs: garnish -> sauce -> herb oil -> final chopped salad.
- Strawberries: breakfast jars -> snack tray -> sweet/savory salad accent.

## Branch Rules

These are the chef moves the pipeline should look for while building a menu.

If making an emulsion:

- Split the base before seasoning it too specifically.
- Homemade mayo can become lemon aioli, green goddess dressing, chili mayo, herby sandwich spread, or a thinned salad dressing.
- If the blender/immersion blender is already dirty, also make the next smooth sauce: hummus, edamame smash, tahini sauce, or herb yogurt.

If boiling salted water:

- Blanch green vegetables before cooking pasta, grains, or noodles.
- Shock and hold some asparagus, peas, edamame, or green beans for salads, frittata, bowls, or toast.
- Use the same pot sequence: vegetables first, starch second, eggs last if needed.

If the oven is hot:

- Roast more than dinner requires, but branch by texture and seasoning.
- Keep one tray neutral with oil and salt.
- Make one tray assertive with harissa, miso, sumac, or chili crisp.
- Toast pita, nuts, seeds, or breadcrumbs at the end on residual heat.

If the cutting board is full of herbs:

- Wash and dry the whole bunch.
- Use stems in sauces or cooked bases.
- Keep delicate leaves for garnish.
- Turn the final handful into herb oil, chopped salad, or yogurt sauce before they fade.

If cooking chickpeas or beans:

- Split before seasoning.
- Some become hummus or smash.
- Some stay whole for salads.
- Some get crisped for crunch.
- Some become patties, falafel, tacos, or pita filling.

If slicing watery crunchy produce:

- Make three states at once: raw snack, salted salad, quick pickle.
- Cucumber, radish, cabbage, fennel, and carrots are especially good for this.

If making a creamy sauce:

- Keep it thick for dip on day one.
- Thin it into dressing on day two.
- Use it as pasta/noodle sauce on day three.
- Fold the last bit into egg salad, chickpea salad, potato salad, or a sandwich spread.

## Prep Primitives

These are the reusable things to make, not standalone recipes.

- Lemon-herb vinaigrette: lemon, sherry or white wine vinegar, olive oil, sumac, salt, chopped mint/parsley/cilantro.
- Miso-tahini sauce: tahini, white miso, lemon or lime, water, sesame oil, maple/agave if needed.
- Yogurt herb sauce: yogurt, lemon, grated garlic, chopped herbs, salt.
- Cucumber-radish quick pickle: sliced cucumber/radish, vinegar or lime, salt, tiny sugar.
- Edamame smash: blanched edamame, lime, cilantro/mint, olive oil, salt, optional miso/tahini.
- Chickpea base: cooked/canned chickpeas seasoned once, then split into salad, falafel/hummus, crispy topping.
- Breakfast base: chia or oats with milk, vanilla, maple, strawberries.
- Snack tray: cucumber, radish, strawberries, pita/sourdough, hummus or yogurt-tahini dip.

## Daily Session Order

Use the same cooking order each day:

1. Start oven, water, grain, or legume first.
2. Wash/chop all produce while heat comes up.
3. Identify the active branch: hot oven, boiling water, blender, herb board, sauce base, cooked legumes, or sliced crunch.
4. Cook tonight's dinner.
5. Before cleaning, ask what else should pass through the active branch.
6. Portion tomorrow's lunch before eating dinner.
7. Pack breakfast/snack trays after dinner, while the kitchen is already open.

## Week Menu

### Tuesday, May 5

Dinner: Charred cabbage sourdough plates with tahini-brown-butter or miso-tahini sauce, lemon, sumac, and mint. Add roasted asparagus if it looks good.

Tetris move: the oven is hot, the tahini jar is open, herbs are washed, and cucumber/radish are on the board.

Branch during the session:

- Make one tahini base, then split it: thick sauce for cabbage, thinner dressing for lunch bowls.
- Slice cucumber/radish once, then split it: raw snack, salted salad, quick pickle.
- Wash herbs once, then split them: mint garnish, chopped herb vinaigrette, stems into dressing.
- Make strawberry chia/oat jars for Wednesday and Thursday.
- Roast extra cabbage/asparagus on the same hot oven cycle, keeping some neutrally seasoned.
- Build a snack tray with cucumber, radish, strawberries, and tahini dip.

Tomorrow's lunch: cabbage chickpea bowl or toast with pickles, herbs, and tahini sauce.

### Wednesday, May 6

Dinner: Asparagus, pea, herb, and feta frittata or omelette with a cucumber-radish salad.

Tetris move: boiling water, eggs, herbs, and green vegetables are all active.

Branch during the session:

- Blanch peas, asparagus, and edamame in the same water before dinner assembly.
- Split edamame: some whole for salads, some smashed with lime/herbs for Thursday lunch.
- Split eggs: dinner frittata plus a few hard-boiled eggs if the pot is still active.
- Cook lentils for Monday's salad base.
- Make yogurt herb sauce while the herb board is already out.
- Save frittata squares for breakfast.

Tomorrow's lunch: edamame smash on pita/sourdough with cucumber-radish pickles and herbs.

### Thursday, May 7

Dinner: Miso-asparagus gnocchi with peas, lemon, herbs, and black pepper.

Tetris move: boiling water and miso-tahini sauce both want to become more than one meal.

Branch during the session:

- Use the same water path: blanch any remaining greens, cook gnocchi, then cook couscous or orzo if needed.
- Make one miso-tahini base, then split it: glossy gnocchi sauce, thinner salad dressing, dip for roasted vegetables.
- Build a green salad box: greens, cucumber, radish, herbs, no dressing.
- Refresh snack tray with yogurt herb sauce.

Tomorrow's lunch: asparagus-pea couscous/orzo salad with edamame smash or chickpeas.

### Friday, May 8

Dinner: Warm spinach-artichoke chickpea flatbreads or bowls with lemon yogurt-tahini sauce.

Tetris move: chickpeas, creamy sauce, and flatbread toppings are becoming the weekend mezze setup.

Branch during the session:

- Soak dried chickpeas for Saturday falafel and hummus. If using canned chickpeas, skip this.
- Roast a harissa carrot or cauliflower tray while dinner warms.
- Split chickpeas before saucing: whole for dinner, some reserved plain for hummus/falafel, some for crispy topping.
- Split creamy sauce: thick flatbread spread, thin bowl dressing.
- Make weekend strawberry breakfast jars.
- Chop extra cucumber/radish/herbs for mezze.

Tomorrow's lunch: spinach-artichoke chickpea pita with cucumber crunch.

### Saturday, May 9

Dinner: Falafel and hummus mezze tray with pita, cucumber-radish-herb salad, pickles, strawberries, and tahini sauce.

Tetris move: chickpeas and blender/processor work are the center of the day.

Branch during the session:

- Use soaked chickpeas for falafel.
- Cook a portion of chickpeas for hummus, or use canned chickpeas.
- Before washing the processor, make falafel mix, hummus, and any edamame/herb smash that still makes sense.
- Make enough falafel for Sunday lunch.
- Make cabbage/cucumber/radish slaw from the same crunchy board.
- Save herbs for Sunday pasta.

Tomorrow's lunch: falafel pita or hummus bowl with slaw and pickles.

### Sunday, May 10

Dinner: Zucchini, pea, lemon, mint, and pecorino pasta. If zucchini looks weak, use asparagus and peas instead.

Tetris move: pasta water, herbs, and remaining vegetables become both dinner and Monday's cleanup structure.

Branch during the session:

- Turn remaining herbs into herb oil or loose pesto.
- Cook extra pasta for Monday lunch if useful.
- Roast any remaining vegetables.
- Build Monday snack tray.

Tomorrow's lunch: pasta salad with chickpeas, herb oil, cucumber, and radish.

### Monday, May 11

Dinner: Green pea and lentil salad with yogurt dressing, crispy chickpeas, cucumber/radish, herbs, and pita chips.

Tetris move: this is the rescue board. Everything left gets assigned a final texture.

Branch during the session:

- Crisp remaining chickpeas or pita.
- Thin any leftover creamy sauce into dressing.
- Fold remaining herbs into salad, dressing, or yogurt.
- Use remaining greens, pickles, roasted vegetables, and lentils.
- Make Tuesday breakfast jars.
- List next week's remaining ingredients before shopping.

Tomorrow's lunch: lentil salad bowl or pita pocket.

## Repeatable Algorithm

For future weeks:

1. Score ingredients by seasonality and personal-library frequency.
2. Choose 4 to 6 anchors with overlapping cuisines and textures.
3. Build meals by active branches: oven, pot, blender, herb board, sauce base, legume batch, crunchy board.
4. Prefer branches that create different formats: dip -> dressing -> pasta sauce, beans -> smash -> crispy topping, herbs -> garnish -> sauce.
5. Schedule long-lead work one day before it is needed.
6. Put the most perishable raw greens/herbs early, sturdy vegetables and legumes later.
7. Each dinner should naturally imply tomorrow's lunch.
8. Each session should exploit whatever equipment, heat, or base is already active before cleanup.
9. End the week with a rescue meal: salad, bowl, frittata, pasta, soup, or flatbread.
